// Current progress

The first physical build.

The first design is done. I used a massage back pad and a hair clip to hold the ultrasonic sensor against the chair back, and the breadboard and Arduino sit on the chair arm. Still waiting on the speaker and TF card.

// Motivation

Sitting badly for years sent me to the doctor.

As an engineering student, I spend long hours sitting while studying and working on projects. Over time, this led to noticeable middle back pain and eventually required a doctor visit. That experience made posture feel less like a small habit issue and more like a real health problem worth addressing early. — Project author
  • ✕Most posture gadgets cost a lot, and some lock features behind a subscription.
  • ✕Wearables mean something strapped to you all day.
  • ✕Camera setups watch you at your desk — and that's a privacy problem.
  • ✓Posture Pilot just measures distance against a baseline you set once.

// System architecture

From the sensor to the reminder.

The HC-SR04 sits on the chair back and measures the gap to your spine. The Arduino filters those readings and decides whether you're slouching. If you are, it lights up the screen and triggers the speaker.

Good posture

Back stays near the chair — sensor reads ~14 cm.

Slouching

Leaning forward off the backrest — gap grows to ~22 cm.

// Detection logic

It checks every reading against your baseline.

Once you've calibrated, good posture just means the reading stays near your baseline. Slouch forward and the gap opens up. Drag the slider to run the same check the firmware runs.

Filtered samplingIt averages a handful of readings and trims the outliers before changing state, so a twitch or a noisy ping won't trip it.

Sustained-slouchThe warning only fires after several slouchy readings in a row — reaching for your coffee won't set it off.

// Calibration · a real sequence

One press sets your personal baseline.

Sit upright in your chair.

Press the LCD shield SELECT button.

The current distance is stored as your baseline posture.

The system announces "Calibration complete."

Continuous posture monitoring begins.

› The baseline comes from several filtered readings, not one raw ping — so it holds steady.

Recommended support parts for the MP3 module

  • 1k Ω resistor between Arduino D11 and MP3 RX
  • 100 µF capacitor across MP3 VCC and GND
  • 0.1 µF ceramic bypass capacitor near the module power pins
  • FAT32-formatted microSD / TF card

// Demo script

Run the demo

  1. Power on and wait for the startup screen and startup audio.
  2. Press the LCD shield SELECT button while sitting upright.
  3. Show the calibration-complete screen and audio prompt.
  4. Sit with good posture and show the stable Posture Good status.
  5. Deliberately slouch for several seconds to trigger sustained-slouch detection.
  6. Let the system play the posture warning audio.
  7. Explain the multi-sample filtering behind each decision.

// Testing notes

Before final assembly

  • Calibration repeats multiple times without a power reset.
  • HC-SR04 returns stable readings at the expected back-to-chair distance.
  • TF card is readable and correctly formatted.
  • Audio files named exactly 0001–0004.mp3.
  • Both brief shifts and sustained slouching tested so the threshold can be tuned.
